Hemoglobin reference ranges define normal blood protein levels, varying primarily by sex and age. Standard adult reference values typically span 13.8 to 17.2 g/dL for men and 12.1 to 15.1 g/dL for women. [1, 2]  
Hemoglobin is an iron-rich protein found inside red blood cells that transports oxygen from the lungs to body tissues. Laboratory tests measure this protein in grams per deciliter (g/dL) to evaluate overall blood health. [3, 4, 5]  
Values below the reference range indicate anemia, which can stem from iron deficiency, blood loss, or chronic illness. Conversely, levels above the reference range may signal polycythemia, often caused by dehydration, smoking, or high-altitude living. Because precise cutoffs differ slightly by specific laboratory methods, medical professionals interpret these numbers alongside other complete blood count metrics. [1, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10]
