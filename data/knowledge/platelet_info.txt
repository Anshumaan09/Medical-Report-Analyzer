A platelet count reference range of 100,000 to 200,000 platelets per microliter (μL) represents a slightly lower-than-standard or borderline platelet concentration in human blood. While standard laboratory reference numbers typically run from 150,000 to 450,000 /μL, a count of 100,000–200,000 /μL sits at the lower threshold and often warrants clinical observation. [1, 2, 3]  
Clinical Context and Meaning 

• Mild Thrombocytopenia Risk: The lower end of this range (&lt;150,000/μL) crosses into mild thrombocytopenia, meaning blood has fewer clotting cells than optimal. 
• Normal Variation vs. Suppression: Some healthy individuals naturally maintain lower baseline counts, but values near 100,000 /μL can signal early bone marrow suppression, medication side effects, or mild viral infections. 
• Monitoring Requirements: Doctors assess this range alongside other Platelet Blood Test (PLT) markers like Mean Platelet Volume (MPV) to determine if low counts stem from underproduction or active destruction. [9]
